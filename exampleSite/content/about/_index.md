# About Sigil

Sigil is a theme for people who write more than they publish. It is built for
long-form essays, research notes, reading logs, and digital gardens — the kind
of site that grows into an archive rather than a feed.

## Why this exists

Most themes are optimized for scanning. Sigil is optimized for reading. The
body sits at 18px on a 720px measure, line-height 2.05, justified like a
printed page. Footnotes become Tufte-style sidenotes on wide screens and
collapse to endnotes on narrow ones, so a citation never pulls your eye off
the sentence.

The palette is paper and ink, with three accents and nothing else: vermilion
for what responds, ink-green for what informs, amber for what glows. A single
∴ marks the end of a section. There is no carousel, no hero banner, no
"Get started" button standing between you and the first paragraph.

## Who it's for

You, if you keep notes you intend to reread. A personal essayist, a graduate
student's notebook, a literary archive, a small journal — anyone who would
rather read their own site than scroll it. If you post daily and need a feed
optimized for engagement, a lighter theme will serve you better, and that is
a reasonable choice rather than a failure of the software.

## What's here

This demo doubles as its own documentation. The [typography sample](/posts/typography/)
exercises every feature — sidenotes, math, diagrams, code, three languages in
one family — so you can judge the typesetting before installing. Read
[Documentation](/docs/) when you are ready. The essays in the [archives](/archives/)
are here to show how real prose sits on the page.
